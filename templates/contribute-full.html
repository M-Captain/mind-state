1. Introduction and Purpose

1.1 About MindState

MindState is a student-led, members-only editorial community for honest conversations about mental health, culture, society and being human. We publish articles (essays, personal essays, poems, fiction, lifestyle pieces) and art posts in English and German.

1.2 Why these guidelines exist

Our readers often come to MindState when they are feeling vulnerable. These guidelines protect them, protect our contributors, and keep the platform trustworthy, legal and consistent. They are friendly in tone but they are not optional: by contributing to MindState you agree to follow them.

1.3 Who these guidelines apply to

  • Members who write, draw, translate or comment.
  • The MindState editorial team, including translators and editors.
  • The MindState admin team, who review submissions.
  • The core tech team and all tech contributors, who build and maintain the platform.

1.4 How to read this document

Must / must not means a firm rule; breaking it can lead to removal of content or membership. Should means strongly recommended. Anything marked to be confirmed by the core team is our current best understanding and will be confirmed before it becomes binding.

Some rules mention platform features that are not built yet (for example member profiles, dashboards, the submission flow, the admin review dashboard, the Feedback & Requests space, the collaboration space, the illustrations contribution page and the language toggle). These are listed as Upcoming Features in Section 12 and will be built collaboratively by the whole team. The rules themselves apply now, and they continue to apply as each feature launches. Until a feature exists, the MindState editorial and admin teams handle that step directly.

Important: MindState is not therapy, diagnosis or crisis support. Nothing published on MindState replaces professional help.

2. Community Values and Code of Conduct

2.1 Our values

  • Honesty: we share real experiences and ideas, in plain words.
  • Care: we write and respond with the reader's wellbeing in mind.
  • Respect: we respect every person's identity, language, culture and experience.
  • Credit: we always credit the people whose work we use.
  • Human craft: our words, illustrations and translations are made by people.

2.2 Expected behaviour

  • Be kind and constructive in comments, reviews and collaborations.
  • Respect people's right to anonymity. Never try to identify an anonymous author.
  • Use content warnings and handle sensitive topics responsibly.
  • Give and accept editorial feedback in good faith.

2.3 Not allowed

  • Harassment, hate speech, discrimination, threats or bullying.
  • Content that encourages self-harm, suicide, disordered eating or substance misuse, or that gives instructions for them.
  • Sharing someone's private information (doxxing), including revealing an anonymous author.
  • Plagiarism, publishing work that is not your own, or using images without an open licence and credit.
  • AI-generated illustrations, or machine-translated text.
  • Spam, advertising or self-promotion unrelated to MindState.

2.4 Consequences

Depending on how serious it is, a breach may lead to: a note and request for changes; removal of the content; temporary suspension; or permanent removal from MindState. Decisions are made by the admin team and can be appealed once through the Feedback & Requests space (Section 7).

3. Contributor Rights and Responsibilities

3.1 Right to anonymity

Contributors have the right to be anonymous. You may publish under a pen name or as "Anonymous". Your public profile and your posts will never show your real name unless you choose that.

Anonymity is also protected from admins:

  • An anonymous author's real identity is visible to only one or two named admins.
  • Every lookup of an anonymous author's identity is logged (who looked, when and why).
  • Identity may only be looked up for safety, legal or serious moderation reasons, never out of curiosity.
  • Revealing an anonymous author's identity without consent is a serious breach of these guidelines.

3.2 Original work only

No one may publish an article that isn't their own. The same applies to art. You must be the author or artist of what you submit. Quotes must be short, clearly marked and credited. Collaborations are allowed only as credited dual contributions (Section 6).

3.3 Member profiles and personal dashboard

Upcoming feature: Profiles and personal dashboards are not built yet (Section 12.2). The rule applies now: everyone who posts needs a member profile, set up with the MindState team until the feature launches.
  • Member profiles are mandatory for everyone who posts.
  • Photos are not required. You may use no photo, an initial, or an illustration you own.
  • Your profile shows your posts. Anonymous posts are not linked to your public identity.
  • Every member has a personal dashboard with views and stats for their own posts.

3.4 Consent and licence at submission

When you submit, you must tick a consent and licence checkbox confirming that:

  • the work is your own (or a credited collaboration);
  • every image is openly licensed and its source is linked; and
  • MindState may publish the work on the platform (including its translation and audio version).

You keep ownership of your work. Licensing terms for publication are to be confirmed by the core team.

4. Content Guidelines: Posting Articles and Art

4.1 How to post an article

Upcoming feature: The online submission flow and status tracker are upcoming features (Section 12.2 and 12.4). Until they launch, follow the same steps with the MindState editorial team, who will submit and track your piece with you. The rules in each step apply now.

1. Sign in and make sure your member profile is complete (name or pen name, short bio; photo optional).

2. Start a new Article submission. Choose the type (essay, personal essay, poem, fiction, lifestyle or other) and the language (English or German).

3. Add a title, a short summary and your text. Write in your own words.

4. Add a content warning (required; write "None" only if truly none applies).

5. Add up to 5 images, mark exactly 1 as featured, and add the source link and licence for every image.

6. Choose whether to publish under your name, a pen name or anonymously.

7. Tick the consent and licence checkbox and submit.

8. Follow your submission in the status tracker (Draft, Submitted, In review, Changes requested, Approved, Published). Respond to admin notes if changes are requested.

9. After approval, the editorial team arranges the manual translation and the post is scheduled for publication.

4.2 How to post art

1. Make sure your member profile is complete.

2. Start a new Art submission and choose the language of your descriptions (English or German).

3. Upload up to 5 images of your work. Illustrations must be hand-drawn (Section 4.5).

4. For each image, write a description and its meaning (Section 4.6).

5. Add a content warning (required) and any tags or themes.

6. Tick the consent and licence checkbox and submit.

7. Track the review in the status tracker. The editorial team translates your descriptions manually before publication.

4.3 Article posts: image rules

  • Maximum 5 images per article.
  • Exactly 1 featured image. An article cannot be published with zero or more than one featured image.
  • Every image must follow the licensing and credit rules in Section 4.7.
  • Every image should have alt text describing it for screen-reader users.

4.4 Art posts: image rules

  • Maximum 5 images per art post.
  • Each image must have a description and its meaning.
  • Each image should also have short alt text for accessibility.

4.5 Illustrations must be hand-drawn

Rule: Illustrations must be hand-drawn by people. They cannot be made with AI. AI may only be used to digitise them, without drastic change.
  • Allowed: drawing on paper or digitally by hand (e.g. with a tablet and stylus), then scanning or photographing it.
  • Allowed: using tools (including AI-based tools) only to digitise the work: cleaning up a scan, removing background, correcting colour, light or perspective, without drastic change.
  • Not allowed: generating, extending, restyling or substantially altering an illustration with AI; AI "filters" that change the style or content; submitting AI art as your own.
  • The admin team may ask for process evidence (sketches, original files or photos of the original). Work that appears AI-generated will not be published.

Illustrations for the platform itself

Members can also create illustrations that will be used in different sections and areas of the MindState platform (for example page headers, section dividers, empty states, the About and Community pages, buttons and small spot illustrations), to make MindState playful and good looking.

  • The same rules apply: platform illustrations must be hand-drawn by people, and AI may only be used to digitise them without drastic change.
  • Every platform illustration must have its meaning explained in English or German, translated manually by the editorial team (Section 4.6).
  • Platform illustrations should fit the MindState design system (Section 10): warm, hand-drawn, sketch or crayon feel, using the colour tokens where possible.
  • Platform illustrations are credited to their artist (by name, pen name or anonymously) on the page where they appear or on a credits page.
  • Platform illustrations are not posted like articles or art posts. They have their own Illustrations contribution page (an upcoming feature), where members upload them. The admin and core tech teams review them there and decide where each one is placed.

4.6 Explaining illustrations: English or German, always translated

  • Every illustration's meaning must be explained properly in English or German. A single word or caption is not enough: explain what it shows and what it means.
  • The explanation must be translated (DE to EN, or EN to DE) so all readers can understand it.
  • The translation is done manually by the MindState editorial team (Section 5). Do not supply machine translations.

4.7 Image licensing and credits

Rule: No images unless open source / openly licensed. Credit with a link to the source is mandatory.
  • Allowed sources: your own work; public domain; openly licensed images (e.g. Creative Commons licences that allow reuse, Unsplash-style free licences). Check the licence terms every time.
  • Every image needs: creator name, source link, and licence, shown with the image or in the post credits.
  • Not allowed: images found via search engines without a clear licence, screenshots of other people's work, stock images you have not licensed, or images with watermarks.
  • The image licence checker blocks publishing if any image is missing its source link or licence field (upcoming feature, Section 12.7).

4.8 Content warnings and sensitive topics

  • A content warning is a required field on every submission (e.g. self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, abuse, substance use, grief, violence).
  • Avoid graphic descriptions of methods of self-harm or suicide. Focus on experience, recovery, support and hope.
  • Pieces on sensitive topics may have comments switched off or pre-moderated (Section 12.3).
  • Where relevant, the post will show the crisis resources bar with helplines in Germany and India.

5. Translation Policy

Rule: Translation of articles, art descriptions, etc. must NOT use AI, Google Translate, or similar services. It is done manually by the MindState editorial team.

5.1 What this covers

This rule applies to all text: articles, poems, art descriptions and meanings, titles, summaries, captions, content warnings and image credits. It covers every direction: English to German and German to English.

5.2 How translation works

  • Approved pieces enter the translation queue (upcoming feature, Section 12.5) with an assignee and deadline.
  • A member of the editorial team translates the piece by hand.
  • Where possible, a second editor checks the translation, and the author may review it.
  • The translation is published as the human translation behind the post's language toggle.

5.3 Narrow exception: audio versions only

Exception: Online services (e.g. Google text-to-speech) may be used ONLY for voice narration, and ONLY on text that the editorial team has written or translated manually, read exactly as published WITHOUT ANY CHANGES. These services must not translate, rewrite, summarise or edit anything. This exception does not allow any automated text translation. Text translation always stays manual, as required by Section 5.

In practice: the audio version is a voice narration of the published text, word for word. No online or automated service may create, translate, rewrite, summarise or edit the text itself.

6. Collaboration Between Artists and Writers

Upcoming feature: The collaboration space and dual-contribution publishing are not built yet (Section 12.2). Until then, artists and writers can arrange collaborations through the editorial team; the rules below apply now.

MindState has a collaboration space where artists and writers can find each other and work together. For example, an artist makes the featured image for a writer's article, or a writer writes a piece inspired by an artist's work.

  • Collaborations are published as a dual contribution with both people credited.
  • All other rules still apply: hand-drawn illustrations only, max 5 images, exactly 1 featured image for articles, descriptions and meanings for art, manual translation.
  • Both contributors must agree to the final version and both tick the consent and licence checkbox.
  • Either contributor may choose to be credited anonymously or with a pen name.
  • Disagreements are resolved with help from the editorial team.

7. Feedback and Requests Space

Upcoming feature: The Feedback & Requests space is not built yet (Section 12.2). Until it launches, send feedback and requests through the contact options on the About page, and report tech bugs as GitHub issues.

The Feedback & Requests space is where members can:

  • Report bugs on the platform.
  • Suggest features.
  • Request a poem, article or art piece on a topic.
  • Appeal a moderation decision (once per decision).

Each item is visible to the relevant departments (for example, bugs to the core tech team, content requests to the editorial team). Please be specific, kind, and do not include other people's private information.

Upcoming feature: The admin review dashboard is not built yet (Section 12.2). Until then, the admin team reviews submissions directly, following the same approve/disapprove-with-notes rules.

The MindState admin team uses an admin dashboard to:

  • Review all submissions.
  • Approve or disapprove each submission.
  • Add notes on disapproval, explaining what needs to change (notes are shown inline in the contributor's status tracker).
  • See views and platform details.

Access depends on role. Admins only see what their role needs. Access to anonymous authors' identities is restricted to one or two admins and logged (Section 3.1).

8.3 Editorial team

The editorial team edits, schedules and manually translates content, manages the translation queue and editorial calendar, and works with the admin team on approvals.

9. Technical Contribution Guidelines

9.1 What tech members can contribute

  • Bug fixes and small improvements, ideally linked to an item in the Feedback & Requests space or an issue on GitHub.
  • New features from the approved roadmap (Section 12), once assigned or agreed with the core tech team.
  • Accessibility, performance and security improvements.
  • Documentation, tests and developer tooling.
  • Design implementation that follows the design system (Section 10).

Not allowed without core-team approval: new frameworks or large dependencies, changes to authentication or permissions, changes to data models or migrations, new third-party services (especially analytics or tracking), and visual changes outside the design system.

9.2 Required GitHub knowledge

All tech members work through GitHub and must know how to use it properly. Before your first contribution, you must be comfortable with everything on this checklist:

  • Creating a GitHub account with two-factor authentication (2FA) switched on.
  • Cloning a repository and keeping your local copy up to date (git pull / fetch).
  • Creating and switching branches.
  • Staging, committing with clear messages, and pushing a branch.
  • Opening a pull request (PR) with a clear description, screenshots for visual changes, and linked issues.
  • Responding to review comments and pushing follow-up commits.
  • Resolving merge conflicts (rebase or merge from main).
  • Reading CI checks and Vercel preview links on a PR.
  • Using issues and labels.
  • Never committing secrets (.env files, keys, passwords) and knowing what .gitignore does.

9.3 Basic Git workflow

1. Pick or open an issue and agree the scope with the core tech team.

2. Update main locally, then create a branch using the naming rules below.

3. Make small, focused commits with clear messages (e.g. "fix: correct image credit link on art page").

4. Run the linter and test locally before pushing.

5. Push your branch and open a pull request into main. Fill in the description, add screenshots for UI changes.

6. A Vercel staging preview is created for the PR; the core team reviews the code and the preview.

7. Address review comments. At least one core tech team member must approve before merge.

8. A core tech team member merges the PR. Delete the branch afterwards.

9.5 Coding conventions

General

  • Keep changes small and focused; one concern per pull request.
  • Use clear, descriptive names; write comments only where the reason is not obvious.
  • Do not add new dependencies without core-team approval.
  • User-facing text is English and German content supplied by people; never machine-translate UI or content strings.

Django (live site)

  • Follow PEP 8 for Python; use Django templates and template inheritance for shared header and footer.
  • Keep styles in the existing CSS files and reuse existing CSS variables and classes.
  • Database changes only through Django migrations, reviewed by the Backend Lead.
  • Linters/formatters (e.g. Black, Ruff) are to be confirmed by the core team.

Next.js repo (mindstate-v1)

  • TypeScript in strict mode; no plain .js files (allowJs is false).
  • Use the @/ import alias for src/ imports.
  • Pass npm run lint with no errors before opening a PR.
  • Plain CSS with the existing custom properties; no new styling framework without approval.
  • Prefer server components; add "use client" only where interactivity needs it (to be confirmed by the core team).

9.6 Security and privacy rules

  • Production must never run with DEBUG enabled. Debug mode must be off in every production deployment (in Django: DEBUG = False, with ALLOWED_HOSTS set correctly).
  • Never commit secrets. Use environment variables; .env files stay out of Git.
  • Use least-privilege access for admin and hosting accounts; enable 2FA everywhere.
  • Anonymous authors' identities are stored securely, visible to one or two admins only, and every lookup is logged.
  • Use privacy-friendly analytics (Plausible or Umami), not Google Analytics, to respect GDPR in Germany.
  • Report security issues privately to the Security & Privacy Lead, not in public issues.

9.7 Accessibility

  • Use semantic HTML (headings in order, landmarks, real buttons and links).
  • All images need alt text (decorative images use empty alt).
  • Keep visible keyboard focus and full keyboard access.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion (the existing marquee already does).
  • Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA colour contrast, especially for muted taupe text on cream.

10.9 Imagery and iconography

  • Warm, moody editorial photography from openly licensed sources (e.g. Unsplash), credited per image.
  • Public-domain engravings (e.g. Wellcome Collection) and classic paintings on beige (#e9ddc8).
  • Hand-drawn sketch, crayon and ink illustrations by members.
  • Minimal line icons (search, arrows).
  • Logo: round yellow sun with a smile over a green leaf/bowl (36×40px in header and footer), with the MINDSTATE wordmark in Instrument Serif.

10.10 Voice and tone

Warm, student-led, sincere, plain words. Talk to people, not at them. Examples from the site:

  • "A space for honest conversations"
  • "Welcome back to MindState."
  • "Subscribe so you don't miss out!"
  • "Help shape MindState."
  • "We'd love to hear from you."
  • "Accounts are managed by the MindState team."

Always keep the disclaimer visible where relevant: MindState is not therapy, diagnosis or crisis support.

10.11 Design system rules for contributors

  • Reuse the existing colours, fonts, weights, radii, spacing and CSS variables in this section. Do not introduce new ones without Design System Lead approval.
  • New components must follow the specifications in 10.8 (dark pill buttons, outline pills, borderless image cards, rounded dark panels).
  • Respect the breakpoints in 10.6 and test every visual change at each one.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion for any new animation; keep motion short (0.2-0.25s) like the existing transitions.
  • Every visual change must include before/after screenshots in the PR and be checked on the staging preview.
  • Only a few hover states exist today; propose new ones through a PR to the Design System Lead (to be confirmed by the core team).
  • The Next.js repo uses DM Sans instead of Google Sans Flex; align to the official design system (to be confirmed by the core team).

12. Upcoming Features (Roadmap)

The features in this section are not built yet. They form a roadmap that the whole MindState team builds together, collaboratively. They are separate from the current platform described in Section 11.

Rules apply now: The policy rules in Sections 2 to 9 apply now, and they continue to apply as each feature launches. Where a rule depends on a feature that is not live yet, the MindState editorial and admin teams carry out that step directly until the feature is ready.

12.1 How to contribute to upcoming features

1. Pick or propose a feature through a GitHub issue. Check existing issues first; to propose something new, describe the problem, the idea and which rule or roadmap item it supports.

2. Discuss it with the core tech team in the issue. Agree the scope, the approach and who works on it before writing code.

3. Work on a branch named with the correct prefix (e.g. feature/submission-tracker), following Sections 9 and 10.

4. Open a pull request for core-team review, with a description, screenshots and a staging preview. No direct pushes to main.

Writers, artists and editors are welcome to contribute too: share ideas, user feedback and test new features on their staging previews. Priorities and timelines are to be confirmed by the core team.

12.2 Core community features

  • Member profiles (mandatory for everyone who posts; photo optional) showing each member's posts, and a personal dashboard with views and stats (Section 3.3).
  • Submission flow for articles and art, with the required fields and checks in Section 4.
  • Admin review dashboard: review all submissions, approve or disapprove, add notes on disapproval, see views and platform details, with role-based access (Section 8.2).
  • Feedback & Requests space: report bugs, suggest features, request a poem/article/art on a topic, visible to the relevant departments (Section 7).
  • Collaboration space for artists and writers, published as dual contributions with both credited (Section 6).
  • Illustrations contribution page: a separate page (not the article or art post flow) where members upload hand-drawn illustrations for use in different sections and areas of the platform, with the artist credit and the meaning in EN/DE; admins review them and choose where each is placed (Section 4.5).
  • EN/DE language toggle on every post that switches to the human translation when one exists.

12.3 Safety and wellbeing

  • Content warnings (required field) on every submission, shown before the content.
  • Crisis resources bar: a fixed "need help now?" link to helplines in Germany and India.
  • Gentle moderation: reactions such as "this helped me" instead of likes. Comments switched off or pre-moderated on sensitive pieces.
  • Anonymity protection from admins too: an anonymous author's real identity is visible to only one or two admins, and every lookup is logged.

12.4 Contributors

  • Submission status tracker: Draft, Submitted, In review, Changes requested, Approved, Published, with admin notes shown inline.

  • Contributor badges and a yearly credits page.

12.5 Editorial workflow

  • Translation queue: a board of pieces waiting for EN to DE or DE to EN translation, with assignee and deadlines.
  • Editorial calendar for publish dates and themed months (e.g. Mental Health Awareness Week).

12.6 Readers

  • A language toggle on every post that switches to the human translation when one exists.
  • Reading time and an audio version. Online services (e.g. Google text-to-speech) may be used ONLY for voice narration, and ONLY on text that the editorial team has written or translated manually, read exactly as published WITHOUT ANY CHANGES. These services must not translate, rewrite, summarise or edit anything. Text translation stays manual (Section 5.3).
  • A weekly newsletter digest by rubric.
  • An accessibility mode: larger text, dyslexia-friendly fonts, reduced motion.

12.7 Platform and tech

  • Privacy-friendly analytics (Plausible or Umami) instead of Google Analytics, for GDPR in Germany.
  • Image licence checker that blocks publishing without a source link and licence field.
  • Staging previews per pull request on Vercel, reviewed by the core team before merging.

13. Reporting, Enforcement and Changes

13.1 Reporting a problem

Report content, behaviour or safety concerns through the Feedback & Requests space or by contacting the admin team. If someone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services (112 in Germany and India).

13.2 Enforcement

The admin team enforces these guidelines as set out in Section 2.4. The core tech team enforces the technical rules in Section 9 through code review.

13.3 Changes to these guidelines

Only the MindState administration team can approve changes to these guidelines. Proposed changes go through the Feedback & Requests space. The website Guidelines page must always match the latest approved version of this document.
