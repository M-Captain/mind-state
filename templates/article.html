For the men’s category, Japan’s Toa Sasaki pulled off an impressive Caballerial fakie nosegrind, earning the highest score in the Best Trick section and securing the championship. Argentina’s Matias Dell Olio finished second, closely followed by Colombian skater Juancarlos Gonzalez, who also achieved a career-high performance at this event.

“It was amazing to skate alongside my heroes and be on the podium. I’ve learned so much from this experience, and I’m ready to keep pushing forward.”

A participant described the energy in Hawaii as “electric,” highlighting the unique, supportive spirit within the skateboarding community, even at the top competitive levels. The championship showcased not only the skaters’ skills but also their resilience and dedication, setting the stage for the upcoming World Skate Park Championships.

Image By Unsplash

This World Championship was not just about competition but also camaraderie. Athletes and fans celebrated skateboarding culture, from supporting each other’s performances to engaging in skate-related events throughout the city. The championship also highlighted the diversity within the sport, with competitors from 48 nations, all bringing unique styles and skills to the competition. The presence of so many new and seasoned faces exemplifies the growing reach and appeal of skateboarding worldwide.

For fans and athletes alike, the Hawaii championship set the stage for the upcoming World Skate Park World Championship in Ostia, where even more skateboarders will showcase their skills. The high-energy Hawaii competition has left the skateboarding community eager to see what’s next, proving that the sport continues to evolve and excite.
